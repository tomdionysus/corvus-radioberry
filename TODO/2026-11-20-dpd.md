# Transmit predistortion in the gateware

A plan, not started. Branch `item/dpd`. Each stage ends at a gate; nothing
after stage 0 starts until stage 0's measurement says there is distortion
worth removing and a feedback signal good enough to remove it with.

## Aim

A cleaner transmitted signal for modes whose amplitude varies (SSB, PSK,
multi-tone data), for every client, with nothing for the client to do:
the gateware measures the amplifier through the radio's own receiver and
corrects the samples on their way to it. Constant-envelope modes (FT8,
WSPR, FM) and CW gain nothing and are left alone.

Not the aim: PureSignal compatibility. That is a host-side scheme (the
client's DSP does the work and the radio only returns a feedback
receiver); it could be offered separately by routing the feedback to a
receive slot, and is not part of this plan.

## What is known, and what is not

- The transmit chain: 48 ksps I/Q from the Pi (16 bits each), the
  interpolating filter (`tx_fir.sv`, by 8 to 384 ksps), the CIC
  (`tx_cic.sv`, by 200 to 76.8 Msps), the up-converter (`tx_upconv.sv`),
  the AD9866's 12-bit DAC, the PA board, then whatever amplifier follows.
  CW from the key jack joins after the CIC and does not pass the samples'
  path.
- The radio clock gives 1600 clocks for each 48 ksps sample, so every
  calculation below can run serially on one or two multipliers.
- Room in both images after 0.7.0: 8 of 66 memory blocks, 60 of 132
  multiplier elements, about 10 600 of 24 624 logic elements.
- While keyed, a receiver tuned to the transmit frequency sees the
  transmitted tone: -22.7 to -61 dBFS across the keyed runs of
  2026-11-19 and 20, a 26 dB spread between boards and sessions with the
  same 12 W out. The path it arrives by is not known (leakage on the
  board, not a designed coupling), and it is taken before any amplifier
  that follows the PA board, so it says nothing about that amplifier.
- Not measured: the transmitter's intermodulation. One two-tone run on
  an old image gave third-order products at -13 to -15 dBc at every drive
  from 64 to 170, which a real amplifier does not do; the test signal is
  suspected (TODO/ACTIVE.md, section 7).
- Not known: whether the board's receive input is connected, and at what
  gain, while it transmits; the loop's delay; how much of the distortion
  is memoryless.

## The method

A memoryless complex-gain table, the plain textbook form.

- Predistorter: each sample x is multiplied by a complex gain G[k], k
  chosen by x's magnitude (64 or 128 bins, interpolated between two
  bins). One table of 36-bit words fits one memory block.
- Feedback: one receiver, tuned by the gateware to the transmit phase
  word while the transmitter is keyed, gives the amplifier's output at
  baseband, r, coherent with x (the same NCO words, so a constant phase).
- Alignment: r is compared with the x of D samples earlier. The digital
  part of D follows from the design (filter and CIC delays at each
  receive rate); the analogue part is a fraction of a sample at HF. A
  fixed whole delay (a short memory of past x) and a fixed fractional
  delay per receive rate, checked by measurement in stage 1.
- Loop gain: one slow complex estimate L of r over x, taken at the
  highest amplitude bins, so that the table is referred to the peak: the
  gain at full drive stays 1 and lower amplitudes are brought into line
  with it. The predistorter then never raises the peak drive above what
  the client's drive setting already gives; its gain is clamped at 1 in
  magnitude plus a small margin.
- Adaptation: for each sample above a floor, the bin's gain moves a small
  step against the error x - r/L (least mean squares, step normalised by
  the bin's amplitude from a small reciprocal table). Convergence in a
  few seconds of speech is the target; the step is chosen in simulation.
- Guards: adapt only while samples are being transmitted and the keying
  state is steady (not the start latency, the hang, CW or the jack); not
  while the ADC has hit full scale or the feedback is below a floor; not
  for a sample following a transmit underrun. The table returns to unity
  on a change of transmit frequency by more than a set amount, of drive,
  of PA enable, and on any fault. Disabled, the samples pass untouched.

What a memoryless table cannot do: correct effects that depend on the
signal's recent past (supply sag, heating, filter group delay). The
general expectation for this method is 10 to 20 dB off the third-order
products; what this transmitter gives is what stage 1 measures.

## Stages

### 0. Measure the transmitter (no gateware change)

Needs the board owner at the radio, each keyed run on his go-ahead with
the setup confirmed.

- A two-tone signal from piHPSDR's own generator into the dummy load, at
  three drives, the spectrum taken on a separate receiver with enough
  attenuation: third- and fifth-order products in dBc, with and without
  the amplifier that follows the PA board.
- The feedback as the receiver sees it during the same runs: level
  against drive, whether the ADC clips, whether it tracks the output.
- If the amplifier after the PA board is to be corrected, a coupler and
  attenuator from its output to the receive input, and the same
  readings.

Gate: products worse than about -35 dBc somewhere in the working range,
and a feedback level that tracks the output without clipping. Otherwise
the plan stops here and the result is recorded.

### 1. Characterise through the radio's own receiver (no gateware change)

A capture tool beside `rbcat` (C++, in `tools/`): keyed in loopback
first (safe), then into the dummy load on the board owner's go-ahead. It
sends a known amplitude-stepped signal, records the receiver tuned to
the transmit frequency, aligns the two by correlation, and prints
amplitude and phase out against amplitude in, the loop delay at each
receive rate, and the scatter about the curve (the part a memoryless
table cannot correct).

Gate: a repeatable curve; the delay equal to the design's figure within
a fraction of a sample; the scatter small against the curve's bend. This
fixes the table's size, the delays and the step.

### 2. The predistorter in the gateware, its table written by the host

- `rtl/core/tx_dpd.sv` between the transmit FIFO and `tx_fir`: magnitude,
  table read, interpolation, complex multiply, saturation; the table
  written through new board registers (0x34 up, free), an enable bit, a
  capabilities bit.
- Driver: the registers passed through; a sysfs attribute or ioctl for
  the table.
- The stage 1 tool computes a table from its capture and loads it.

Gates: disabled, the core's outputs equal 0.7.0's on every clock
(`corecmp`, 0 mismatches); the block against a bit-exact model in
simulation; three seeds of each image meeting timing; the toolchain's
check; receive and loopback checks on both boards; then, with the board
owner, the stage 0 two-tone runs with the table off and on.

This stage alone is a working predistorter, trained on demand from the
Pi. Whether the improvement holds over drive, band and temperature
without retraining decides how much stage 3 is worth.

### 3. Adaptation in the gateware

- The delay memory for x, the loop-gain estimate, the per-bin update and
  the guards, sequenced by one state machine on shared multipliers; the
  feedback receiver's phase word forced to the transmitter's while keyed
  with predistortion on.
- Status registers: converged, frozen and why, the loop gain, the
  largest table correction, counts of updates and of guard trips; the
  table readable.
- Simulation with an amplifier model (amplitude compression and phase
  shift against amplitude, with and without added memory, noise and
  delay error): convergence time, the products before and after,
  behaviour through gaps in speech, key-up, a clipped ADC and a lost
  feedback signal.

Gates: as stage 2, and on the board: the table settling within a few
seconds of a two-tone and of speech, the products at each drive and on
two bands against stage 2's hand-loaded table, no change in output power
on the meter, no growth of the table when the feedback is removed.

Estimated cost, to be confirmed by the fit: two memory blocks, two
18 x 18 multipliers, 600 to 900 logic elements.

### 4. Daemon, module and documentation

- `dpd: false` by default in `config.yaml`, read by `radioberryd` and
  the SoapySDR module; the daemon logs the state at each transmission's
  end; `rbprobe` reports it.
- The receiver the gateware borrows while keyed: say so in the docs; a
  client running full duplex on that receiver loses it during
  transmission with predistortion on.
- docs/pa.md: what it needs (the feedback path), what it does not do,
  how to check it.

### 5. Release

With a release of its own: the checks of every stage on the release
commit's images, both boards, and a contact by the board owner in a
varying-amplitude mode.

## Decisions for the board owner

1. Whether stage 0's bench session happens, and with which amplifier in
   the loop. Everything else waits on its result.
2. Feedback after the PA board only (no extra hardware, corrects the PA
   board alone) or after the following amplifier (a coupler and
   attenuator into the receive input, corrects the whole chain).
3. Whether borrowing a receiver while keyed is acceptable, or a receiver
   of its own is wanted for the feedback (more logic: its mixer, CIC and
   a filter slot).
4. Whether stage 2's host-trained table is enough to ship, with stage 3
   to follow, or the two ship together.

## Risks

- The feedback seen today is leakage of unknown origin with a 26 dB
  spread; it may not be a faithful sample of the output. Stage 0 settles
  it before any gateware is written.
- A predistorter that adapts wrongly makes the signal worse than none.
  The guards, the peak reference and the clamp bound it; the simulation
  cases in stage 3 are there to break it before the board does.
- Memory effects may leave less improvement than the general
  expectation.
- Transmit changes can put a fault on the air: every keyed run is the
  board owner's, into a dummy load first.
